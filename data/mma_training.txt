MMA training combines striking, wrestling, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, cage work, conditioning, and tactical decision-making.

A beginner MMA training week should balance skill development, strength and conditioning, recovery, and sparring intensity. Too much hard sparring can increase injury risk and slow technical progress.

A basic MMA week might include two striking sessions, two grappling sessions, one wrestling or cage wrestling session, two strength and conditioning sessions, and at least one full recovery day.

MMA striking is different from pure boxing or kickboxing because of takedown threats, smaller gloves, stance changes, cage positioning, and ground transitions. A fighter must avoid overcommitting on punches if it exposes them to takedowns.

Wrestling for MMA includes shots, sprawls, underhooks, overhooks, body locks, mat returns, cage pressure, and takedown defence.

Brazilian jiu-jitsu for MMA focuses on positional control, escapes, submissions, ground-and-pound awareness, and getting back to the feet. Some sport BJJ positions are riskier in MMA because strikes are allowed.

Conditioning for MMA should include aerobic base, explosive power, muscular endurance, and sport-specific intervals. Fighters need the ability to recover between bursts of high intensity.

Recovery is part of training. Sleep, nutrition, mobility, hydration, and managing sparring intensity are important for long-term progress.

A fighter should track training volume, injuries, fatigue, technical goals, and sparring notes.
