The Muay Thai clinch is a close-range fighting position used to control posture, land knees, create off-balances, and set up elbows or sweeps.

Good clinch control starts with posture. The fighter should keep their hips under them, spine strong, chin tucked, and forehead or head position used to apply pressure. Pulling only with the arms is weak. Strong clinch control uses the back, shoulders, hips, head position, and foot placement.

The double collar tie is when both hands control behind the opponent's head or neck. It can be used to break posture and land knees. However, if used poorly, it can expose the fighter to body locks, trips, or counters.

Inside control is very important in clinch fighting. A fighter with inside arm position can frame, pummel, control biceps, create space, or attack with knees and elbows.

Knees in the clinch should be thrown with hip drive, posture control, and balance. A fighter should avoid falling forward after kneeing because this can allow the opponent to turn, sweep, or counter.

Common clinch attacks include straight knees, curved knees, short elbows, turns, dumps, and sweeps.

Clinch defence includes posture recovery, framing across the collarbone, swimming for inside control, controlling the opponent's biceps, stepping around, and avoiding being pulled straight down.

In Muay Thai, off-balancing the opponent is as important as landing strikes. A well-timed turn or dump can score and disrupt the opponent's rhythm.
