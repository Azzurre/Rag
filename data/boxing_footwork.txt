Boxing footwork is the foundation of positioning, balance, defence, and attack.

A boxer should move without crossing their feet. When moving forward, the lead foot moves first and the rear foot follows. When moving backward, the rear foot moves first and the lead foot follows. When moving left, the left foot moves first. When moving right, the right foot moves first.

Good footwork keeps the boxer balanced and ready to punch or defend. Overreaching, standing too square, or crossing the feet can make a fighter vulnerable.

The pivot is a key boxing movement. A pivot allows a boxer to create an angle after punching or defending. For example, after throwing a jab-cross, a boxer may pivot away from the opponent's power side.

Ring cutting is the skill of limiting an opponent's movement instead of chasing them. A fighter cuts the ring by stepping laterally to block exits and guide the opponent toward ropes or corners.

Angles are important because attacking from an angle makes it harder for the opponent to defend and counter. A fighter can create angles with pivots, lateral steps, slips, rolls, and exits after combinations.

A common boxing mistake is moving straight back with the chin high. This can expose the fighter to straight punches. Better defensive movement includes stepping off-line, pivoting, blocking, slipping, or exiting at an angle.

Footwork should support punching. A fighter should avoid throwing combinations while their feet are too narrow, crossed, or out of position.
