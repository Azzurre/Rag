Striking basics include stance, guard, footwork, distance management, defence, and combinations.

A good fighting stance should allow balance, movement, defence, and attack. In orthodox stance, the left foot and left hand are forward. In southpaw stance, the right foot and right hand are forward.

The jab is one of the most important punches in boxing, kickboxing, Muay Thai, and MMA. It can be used to measure distance, interrupt an opponent, set up combinations, hide movement, and create openings for power shots.

The cross is usually thrown with the rear hand. Power comes from rotating the hips, turning the rear shoulder, pushing through the rear foot, and keeping balance after the punch.

Hooks are circular punches often used at mid-range or close range. They can target the head or body. A good hook uses hip rotation and a bent elbow rather than a wide arm swing.

Uppercuts are vertical punches used at close range, especially when an opponent lowers their head or enters the pocket.

Basic defence includes keeping the guard high, using head movement, blocking, parrying, slipping, rolling, checking kicks, and moving the feet.

Distance management is the ability to stay at a range where you can hit the opponent while making it difficult for them to hit you. Long range favours jabs, teeps, and kicks. Mid-range favours combinations. Close range favours clinch, knees, elbows, hooks, uppercuts, and takedowns.

A basic striking combination is jab-cross-hook. Another common combination is jab-cross-low kick. In Muay Thai, a common combination is jab-cross-left hook-right low kick.

A fighter should not admire their work after striking. They should exit, angle off, defend, or prepare the next attack.
